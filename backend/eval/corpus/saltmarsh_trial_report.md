Saltmarsh Array Field Trial: Final Report

This report summarises the field trial of the Saltmarsh Array, Meridian Tidal Power's first multi-turbine deployment. Six Kestrel-2 turbines were installed in Saltmarsh Sound in March 2022 and operated until September 2023. The trial was led by engineering lead Arjun Mehta, with environmental monitoring led by Dr. Lena Fischbach. The trial was part-funded by a grant of 6.5 million euros from the Blue Transition Fund of the Coastal Energy Consortium.

Performance. Across the eighteen months of operation, the array achieved an average capacity factor of 41 percent, above the 35 percent target set in the grant agreement. Availability was 94 percent once the blade issue described below was resolved. Electricity was exported through the Dunmore substation.

Blade delamination. In October 2022, after seven months of operation, inspection divers found delamination on one blade of unit 4. The root cause was traced to a single batch of epoxy resin used by Brightwater Composites that had been cured at too low a temperature. The affected blade was replaced in December 2022, and the other seventeen blades were inspected with ultrasound and found to be sound. Following the incident, Brightwater Composites changed its curing process and added a batch-level hardness test before blades leave its factory.

Wildlife monitoring. Passive acoustic monitors recorded harbour porpoise activity around the array throughout the trial. In the first quarter after installation, porpoise detections within 500 metres of the turbines fell by 12 percent compared with the pre-installation baseline. By the third quarter, detections had returned to baseline levels, suggesting that the animals had habituated to the turbines. No collisions were observed on the turbine-mounted cameras.

Recommendations. The trial team recommends expanding the site to 20 turbines as Saltmarsh Phase II, commissioning a study of acoustic deterrent devices before the expansion, and sharing the wildlife monitoring protocol with other consortium members. Phase II depends on additional export capacity at the Dunmore substation.
